Project 02 · Banking app

Banco do Brasil: Smartphone Subscription in the BB App

How to bring a 21-month rental contract into a bank’s app, moving the rules people will live with to before they sign, not after.

RoleProduct Designer
CategoryFinancial services, subscription
ScopeFlow, interaction design, UI
PlatformBB app, mobile
Year2025–2026

Status: the BB channel MVP is live, with subscribers and measured results. The experience inside the BB app, designed in this project, has not launched yet and is being validated with the bank.

Section

Context

A long contract product, in a channel that already knows the customer.

Leapfone offers smartphones on a rental subscription: the customer pays a monthly fee, the device stays with the company and theft and damage protection is included. On the website, price and payment method are the most cited reasons for giving up in the abandoned-cart survey.

The partnership with Banco do Brasil started as a pilot in December 2023 and became a contract in 2025. Every month, the bank tracks two metrics: new subscriptions per cohort and the volume paid on Ourocard credit cards.

Inside the BB app, the starting point changes: the customer is already authenticated, the data already exists and the card already belongs to the customer’s own tax ID. The project designs the complete experience inside the app, from the storefront to the end of the contract, around that advantage.

  • 1 in 5of B2C subscribers pay with a Banco do Brasil card (19.7%)
  • 21months of contract in the BB channel, with upgrades from month 12
  • 22clauses in the rental contract create an obligation, deadline or right
  • 14 + 5screens and states in the navigable prototype

Section

Problem

Three friction points the channel had to solve.

For the customer

They want a new phone for a monthly fee that fits their budget, but only discover the contract rules after signing.

For the business

For BB, every subscription is recurring volume on Ourocard. For Leapfone, every lost order costs acquisition, analysis and support.

Friction points

87%
Orders that never reach payment

On the website, 51,506 of 59,380 orders created in 12 months ended without payment. The mapping does not break this down by cause.

14/22
The rules live in the PDF

Term, cancellation fee, deductible and end of contract do not appear in any email, screen or message before or after signing.

11,346
A refusal does not say what to do

Credit refusals per year on the website, with practically the same message, even when the system already schedules a new analysis in 3 months.

Source: Leapfone B2C journey mapping (website, 12 months), covering the order funnel, terms of use, contracts and CRM. These are website numbers, not BB channel numbers.

Section

What the analysis showed

The evidence changed where the BB app actually helps.

In the abandoned-cart survey (957 responses, 2024–2026), “I would like to pay another way” is the main reason for giving up, at 18%, followed by “it was too expensive”, at 15%. Of the roughly 35 responses that mention BB or Ourocard, 12 talk about the card limit, which the plan used to take up at the start of the pilot.

01
The limit became the argument

The product stopped taking up the card limit. The design turns that change into an argument: it shows up in the banner, the benefits, the comparison and the payment step.

02
No Ourocard, no dead end

Some BB respondents gave up because they did not have the bank’s credit card. The Ourocard rule now appears before the error, in a dedicated state.

03
Rules before acceptance

14 of 22 clauses had no communication, and in the roadmap alignment meeting marketing described the end of contract of BB’s first cohorts as the biggest friction point: some customers did not know they had to return the device. The rules now enter the flow before acceptance and come back in the logged-in area in the month they matter.

04
“No” is not “not yet”

The system has two different doors, refusal and a waitlist with a new analysis in 3 months, and the customer receives almost the same message. “Not yet” now gets a date, a reminder and an alternative path.

The survey is self-reported and answered by people who gave up. The BB cuts come from a small base and work as a qualitative signal, not as statistics. Sources: B2C journey mapping, Leapfone abandoned-cart survey and the roadmap alignment meeting of 09/09/2026.

Section

Design principle

No rule discovered after the click.

The goal was not only to shorten the signup. It was to make the shortest path also the clearest one, because in a 21-month contract frustration shows up at the end.

01
Before the effort

Requirements and rules appear before the customer fills in or pays anything.

02
At the moment it matters

Each clause comes back on the screen of the month it changes something.

03
With the next step

No “no” without saying what to do now.

Section

Decision 01

Decision 01

Sell what fits the monthly budget and what is already included

Problem

The first version of the home, which I designed myself, promised “pay less” and “it’s even cheaper”, comparing the monthly fee with an installment purchase plus insurance. The two sides used different bases for price, term and coverage.

Alternatives

A
Keep the comparison in reais

As in my initial wireframe and the MVP: a strong argument, but with different bases on each side.

B
Compare what changes day to day

Card limit, protection, accessories, upgrades and who owns the device.

Decision

I proposed B. The home opens with the monthly fee and price bands, and the comparison with the installment purchase makes no promise in reais.

Why

Price and payment method are the most cited reasons for giving up in the survey, and among BB customers the card limit comes up repeatedly. Those are points the subscription really solves.

Trade-off: we lose a headline number for the banner. We gain a comparison in which every line can be verified, including the one where buying wins: the device is yours.

Section

Decision 02

Decision 02

Data and card come from the bank, with explicit consent

Problem

Signup and payment method are points where people give up: “I would like to pay another way” is the main reason in the survey. BB customers mention the Ourocard limit, which was taken up at the start of the pilot.

Alternatives

A
Repeat the website flow

Full signup and any credit card under the same tax ID.

B
Use what the bank already knows

Pre-filled data with authorization, and only the customer’s own Ourocard credit cards.

Decision

I proposed B. The payment step states plainly that the limit is not taken up, and explains that only the first monthly payment is reserved until identity is confirmed. BB’s 6-digit password confirms the payment, as in the rest of the app.

Why

The contract rule becomes a filter instead of an error message: third-party and prepaid cards simply do not show up as options.

Trade-off: it depends on a data integration and agreement with BB, and limits payment to Ourocard. People who want to pay another way, the main reason for giving up in the survey, still have no option in this channel.

Section

Decision 03

Decision 03

Separate “no” from “not yet”

Problem

Refusal and waitlist arrive with almost the same message, no date and no next step, even though the system already treats them differently.

Alternatives

A
One message for every negative result

As it works today.

B
Separate refusal from waitlist

Give those who can come back a date, a reminder and an alternative path.

Decision

I proposed B. Approval is also presented as pre-approval.

Why

Someone coming back in 3 months has already made the effort once. Giving them a date turns an exit into a pause.

Trade-off: the reason for a credit or fraud refusal is not shown: that depends on risk and legal. “Pre-approved” is less celebratory, but avoids promising what identity verification can still change.

Section

Decision 04

Decision 04

The rules enter the flow before acceptance

Problem

Term, cancellation fee, deductible, late payment and end of contract only existed in the PDF signed at checkout. The mapping found 14 of 22 clauses with no communication in any channel.

Alternatives

A
Keep the PDF

With a link at checkout.

B
A dedicated step

The contract summarized in seven blocks of plain language, with active acceptance.

Decision

I proposed B, using the public rules of the BB channel: a cancellation fee of 50% of the remaining monthly payments, a deductible of 2.5 or 4 monthly payments, theft reported within 48 hours, and a 2% late fee plus 1% monthly interest.

Why

It is the moment of highest attention in the journey. The hypothesis: a rule read there costs one screen; discovered in month 21, it costs a support call and trust.

Trade-off: the screen is long precisely at the step with the highest intent, and may lower conversion in the short term. The bet is less friction, fewer support calls and fewer disputed returns later.

Section

Decision 05

Decision 05

The contract stays visible after signing

Problem

After activation, the customer cannot see which month they are in, how much it costs to cancel or when upgrades are unlocked. At the end, some only then discover they have to return the device.

Alternatives

A
Rely on date-triggered emails

The email sequence that today covers upgrades in B2C.

B
A logged-in area with the contract as a timeline

Current month, next charge, today’s cancellation fee and the options at the end.

Decision

I proposed B. The end of the contract shows the three options side by side: upgrade, return or negotiate buying the device.

Why

The same rules from acceptance come back in the month they change something. Buying the device comes with a warning that the subscription’s protection ends.

Trade-off: showing today’s fee, which drops every month, may bring cancellations forward near the end. That is why cancellations in the last months are tracked as a guardrail metric.

Trade-offs

The challenge was not hiding the contract’s complexity. It was deciding when each rule appears.

01
Clear rules × short-term conversion

Summary in seven blocks, with the full contract one tap away.

02
Fewer steps × security and anti-fraud

BB’s password confirms the payment; identity comes after, with status per item and resume.

03
Pre-filled data × privacy and consent

An authorization screen with the exact list of what is shared.

04
One-tap payment × card reach

Only Ourocard under the customer’s tax ID: fewer options and fewer refusals for ineligible cards.

05
Knowing the cost to cancel × retention

Today’s fee appears next to upgrade and discount, as a compared choice.

06
A single brand, BB’s × transparency about who operates

The partner company appears in the data authorization, the privacy policy and the invoice note.

Section

Collaboration

Four changes to the design that came from working with other teams.

The monthly pre-authorization left the flow

Constraint: the system reserved the monthly fee on the card days before each due date and retried every day without telling the customer. Discussion: the journey mapping brought this to the roadmap alignment meeting, with product, marketing and fraud. Decision: the sixth-day pre-authorization was eliminated; only the first monthly payment is reserved until identity is confirmed. Result: the payment step explains that reservation and that it is released within 24 hours if the order is not completed.

The end of the contract became a principle

Constraint: marketing described the end of contract of BB’s first cohorts as the biggest friction point. Discussion: in the same meeting, the proposal was to communicate the rules from day one, not only at the end. Decision: contract transparency became the principle of this design. Result: a contract step before acceptance and a logged-in area with the contract as a timeline.

The limit became an argument

Constraint: at the start of the pilot, the plan took up the Ourocard limit, and BB customers mentioned it in the survey. Discussion: the rule was reviewed with BB’s business and sales team, marketing management, product and design. Decision: the product stopped taking up the limit. Result: the design sells the benefit at four points of the flow.

How the work happens

We work in Scrum with the startup’s product and engineering teams. I present the screens in validations with product, business and marketing on both sides of the partnership, adjust with what comes back, and hand off each screen to engineering with its states, rules and dependencies documented.

Section

Brand

BB’s partnership guidelines became a design requirement.

BB defines two communication modes with partners. Because the subscription lives inside the app and is offered to BB customers, the flow follows the exclusive promotion for BB customers mode: BB’s visual identity and tone of voice, with the bank presenting the product.

01
Only the BB brand inside the app

By partnership decision, the partner’s brand does not appear in the app at this stage. If it does later, the guideline’s shared signature applies, with BB always on the right.

02
BB presents the product

The bank speaks in the first person and the partner appears without a brand name. Data authorization and the invoice note say who operates the subscription.

03
BB’s visual universe

Yellow, blue and cyan from the guidelines, diagonal cuts instead of circles and BB’s proprietary typeface.

The final experience

Fourteen stages, four phases, one navigable prototype.

01
Discovery

Entry card in the BB app home, the subscription home with price bands and a qualitative comparison, and the product page with condition, color and the contract as a timeline.

02
Signing

04

Details that matter

Care lives in the small parts.

01
Named steps

Five steps with a name and number: the customer always knows where they are and what is left.

02
The rule becomes a filter

Only credit cards under the customer’s own tax ID appear, so third-party and prepaid cards drop out before becoming an error.

03
The name on the invoice

Without the partner brand in the app, the customer would not recognize the name on the statement. One line prevents “I don’t recognize this charge”.

04
The reservation, explained

The first monthly payment stays reserved until identity is confirmed. Saying so beforehand prevents the feeling of an undue charge.

05
The cost before the decision

Today’s cancellation fee appears in reais, next to the rule that calculates it. Buying the device warns what changes.

06
Contrast and touch

Small text on yellow uses dark ink (15:1), because BB blue only reaches 4.4:1 there. Touch targets are at least 44 px.

Impact and success criteria

What has been measured, and how to measure what comes next.

The measured results belong to the BB channel MVP, which is live. The experience inside the app has not launched yet, so it has no metrics of its own.

  • +16%conversion in the MVP, with credit analysis before payment done with a credit partner
  • +41%subscriptions in the MVP, with the refurbished-device flow in the catalog

How to measure the in-app experience

Above all, it has to move what BB already tracks: new subscriptions per cohort and volume paid on Ourocard.

  • Payment completion. More orders reach confirmed payment? Compared with the current BB channel MVP, which already includes the analysis before payment.
  • Card refusals. Do ineligible or unauthorized cards drop as a reason for losing orders?
  • Identity completion. Do those who pay finish documents and biometrics, per item?
  • Understanding the rules. Can the customer say how long the contract lasts and how much it costs to cancel? A comprehension test with account holders before launch.
  • End-of-contract friction. Fewer support contacts and fewer disputed returns per active subscriber?
  • Waitlist return. Do those who received a date come back for the new analysis?

Guardrail metrics

  • Cancellations in the last months. Showing today’s fee, which drops every month, must not bring exits forward. Tracked per cohort.
  • Unauthorized monthly payments. Preserving the limit must not turn into declined charges.

Section

Reflection

In a long contract product, the experience does not end at payment. The screen that prevents the problem in month 21 has to exist on day 1.

01
Test with account holders

Validate rule comprehension and the contract step before measuring conversion.

02
Build with BB’s components

The interface started from BB’s partnership brand guidelines, the initial wireframe and what the app shows. With access to the app’s components and tokens, the next step is to rebuild with them.

03
Close with risk and legal

What can be said on a refusal, and whether BB’s biometrics can replace the document upload.
