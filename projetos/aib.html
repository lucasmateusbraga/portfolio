Project 01 · Digital banking

Redesigning Pay & Transfer for faster, clearer everyday payments.

I redesigned AIB’s Pay & Transfer journey: payee setup, IBAN validation, instant payments, authentication and what happens when the daily limit is reached. The goal was to make common transfers easier to finish while keeping security and payment status clear.

RoleSenior Product Designer
ScopeDiscovery, product strategy, interaction, UI, prototyping, design system
PlatformiOS & Android
DomainPayments, risk, authentication

AIB MOBILE BANKING

The starting point: AIB’s mobile app for iOS and Android.

Context

Pay & Transfer needed to become easier without weakening the controls around a bank transfer.

More banking activity was moving to mobile and online channels in Ireland, while payment fraud kept security central to the experience. For Pay & Transfer, that meant reducing unnecessary steps in routine transfers without treating higher-risk actions the same way.

Audit findings

The audit showed four interaction problems I could fix.

IBAN errors appeared too late, Pay a Contact sat outside the main transfer path, completed steps gave little feedback, and the daily limit could stop a transfer without a clear recovery path. These became the four areas I focused on.

Risk and friction

I added stronger authentication only when the level of risk changed.

Routine transfers kept the standard confirmation path. A temporary limit increase required stronger authentication because it changed the customer’s exposure.

Trust

I checked what the customer needed to know at each point in the transfer.

Before submission, the customer needs to confirm the recipient and amount. During processing, the product needs to show that the instruction is being handled. Afterward, the outcome and receipt need to be unambiguous.

BEFORE

Am I sending the right money to the right person?

Recipient · IBAN · receiving bank · amount · payment method

DURING

Is the bank actually processing my instruction?

Authentication · locked button · processing status · no duplicate sends

AFTER

Did the money move, and can I prove it?

Result · time · reference · receipt · what to do if unsure

Decision 01

Move IBAN validation into the form.

Problem

The existing form could leave an IBAN problem until submission.

Decision

I moved validation into the form, showed the receiving bank when available and gave the customer a specific correction when the IBAN could not be accepted.

Trade-off

The form gives more feedback while the customer types, and there’s less doubt when they submit.

Decision 02

Bring Pay a Contact into the main recipient flow.

Problem

Pay a Contact existed, but it was separated from the main transfer entry point and required customers to find it first.

Decision

I brought Contact alongside the other recipient options so the journey could start with who the customer wanted to pay.

Trade-off

Starting with contacts means someone paying a new payee without a phone-linked account still needs the manual IBAN path, so that path has to stay.

Decision 03

Add explicit feedback after actions that affect the transfer.

I added feedback where customers needed confirmation that an action had been accepted, was still processing or had completed.

Problem

Several steps previously changed with little confirmation that the action had been accepted.

Decision

The redesign uses contextual feedback, including SEPA Instant availability while entering the amount and a receipt after completion.

Trade-off

More states to build and maintain than one generic loading screen.

Decision 04

Let customers recover from the daily limit without leaving the transfer.

Problem

A permanent limit increase would create more exposure than the immediate transfer required. A hard stop, however, could push a legitimate customer to support.

Decision

I designed a temporary increase that asks for the new amount, requires Face ID and returns to the previous limit automatically after 24 hours.

Trade-off

A temporary increase that reverts automatically could surprise someone who expected it to stay, so the screen says clearly when it ends.

Trade-offs

The main trade-offs came from speed, security and recoverability.

Speed × Security
Flexibility × Fraud exposure
Automation × Customer control
Minimal UI × Explicit feedback

I kept routine actions light and added extra steps when the customer changed the level of risk, such as temporarily increasing the daily limit.

Where the customer could fix the problem, the flow kept the recipient, amount and payment details so they didn’t have to start again.

Uncertain outcome

An interrupted transfer needed a clear status before the customer could try again.

If the connection drops after confirmation, the customer may not know whether the transfer was submitted. I treated that separately from a normal failure and blocked another submission until the transaction status was known.

01Confirm

The customer confirms.

02Lock

The button can’t send twice.

03Process

The transfer is pending.

04Resolve

Check the confirmed status.

05Recover

Retry only when it’s safe.

How I’d measure it

I defined the measures I would use to evaluate the redesigned journey after launch.

The plan focused on transfer completion, time to complete, validation errors, duplicate attempts, limit increases done by the customer and confidence after the transfer. These are criteria for evaluating the redesign. They are not reported results.

MAIN METRIC
Transfers completed without help
01CompletionTransfer completion rate
02SpeedMedian time to complete
03QualityIBAN errors · duplicate attempts
04RecoveryLimit increases done in the app
05ConfidenceConfidence after the transfer / CSAT
What I would watch

I would read completion time together with validation errors, duplicate attempts, support contacts and recovery rate. A shorter flow would not be an improvement if it created more uncertainty or failed transfers.

Reflection

The main lesson was to design status and recovery with the same care as the main flow.

This project changed how I approach financial flows. I now map validation, failure and recovery earlier, because those states also shape the main interaction.
